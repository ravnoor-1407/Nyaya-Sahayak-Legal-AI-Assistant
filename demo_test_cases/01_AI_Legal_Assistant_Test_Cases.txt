TEST CASE 1.1: POLICE ARREST RIGHTS & 24-HOUR MAGISTRATE PRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT TO COPY:
Can police arrest without a warrant in India, and what are my rights at the time of arrest?

EXPECTED CITATIONS:
- BNSS Section 35 (Arrest without warrant)
- BNSS Section 47 (Right to be informed of grounds of arrest & bail entitlement)
- BNSS Section 48 (Right to have family member or relative informed)
- BNSS Section 58 (Mandatory production before Judicial Magistrate within 24 hours)
- Constitution of India Article 22

VERIFICATION POINTS:
- Assistant clearly distinguishes between cognizable and non-cognizable offences.
- Explains mandatory 24-hour presentation before the nearest Magistrate.
- Shows clickable source pills.
- Statutory Audit pill shows 80%+ verified citations.

TEST CASE 1.2: NEW SAFEGUARD FOR SENIOR CITIZENS & PETTY OFFENCES (BNSS 2024)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT TO COPY:
Can police arrest an elderly person or a woman for an offence punishable with less than 3 years?

EXPECTED CITATIONS:
- BNSS Section 35(7)

VERIFICATION POINTS:
- Assistant highlights that under Section 35(7) BNSS, no arrest of a person who is infirm or over 60 years of age can be made without prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police (DSP).
- Explains preliminary inquiry safeguard for offences punishable with 3 to 7 years.

TEST CASE 1.3: ADMISSIBILITY OF WHATSAPP CHATS & DIGITAL EVIDENCE (BSA 2023)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT TO COPY:
What is required to submit a WhatsApp chat or phone recording as evidence in court under the new Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam?

EXPECTED CITATIONS:
- BSA Section 61 (Admissibility of electronic records)

VERIFICATION POINTS:

- Details the mandatory certificate signed by the person in lawful management/charge of the computer system or mobile device.

TEST CASE 1.4: REMEDY IF POLICE STATION REFUSES TO REGISTER FIR (ZERO-FIR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT TO COPY:
What legal remedies are available if a police station refuses to lodge my FIR for theft or assault?

EXPECTED CITATIONS:
- BNSS Section 173(1) (Mandatory Zero-FIR registration irrespective of territorial jurisdiction)
- BNSS Section 173(4) (Sending written complaint by post to Superintendent of Police)
- BNSS Section 175(3) / 175(4) (Application to Judicial Magistrate)

VERIFICATION POINTS:
- Details Zero-FIR: Police cannot turn a citizen away citing lack of jurisdiction.
- Explains postal complaint to SP and subsequent 156(3)/175(3) court application.
